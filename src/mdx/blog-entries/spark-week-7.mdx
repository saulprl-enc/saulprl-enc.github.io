# Spark Week 7
<br />

Week 7 was a week full of ups and downs. To start it off, we were assigned a new project for the next two weeks; a very similar one, but with a twist: wrapping an external API with our own backend. We had a meeting, a _Spark Communication Session_, and I received feedback regarding my demo. As per usual, we'll first dive into the meetings.
<br />

## Meetings
<br />

### Learning Styles
<br />

We were given a talk about **learning styles**, where we got to explore a little about the different ways in which each of us learns, and how it can be beneficial to understand the optimal way for us to do it, identifying different strategies and approaches. In my case, I've always believed I learn the most by doing, so I always focus on actually coming up with little projects or exercises that allow me to dive deeper on a subject.
<br />

### Spark Communication Sessions
<br />

As scheduled, we had a _Spark Communication Session_ on Friday, where we got to talk about a topic for 5 minutes without preparation. I decided to talk about my biggest project for the last year and a half: **CSI PRO ACCESS**, since it's a topic I know really well and it's something I like talking about, having given conferences about it to tens and hundreds of people. I believe I did pretty well because I feel confident about this topic; however, I probably should've gone for something that I could actually talk about **and** conclude in 5 minutes. The feedback I received was really useful, especially when they told me about having an effective hook as a starting point for my talk, and it's something I'll be actively looking to include every time I speak in public from now on.
<br />

### Meeting with my mentor
<br />

Prior to receiving my feedback report for the week, my mentor and I scheduled a quick meeting to review my ToDo List's code and mainly focused on the front-end. I got to explain my reasoning behind some of the decisions made during development, including some of my choice on libraries, and also effectively exchanged knowledge on topics like runtime schema validations, component libraries, and async state. I received some feedback regarding avoiding hardcoding values like the API URL, coming up with better abstractions for reusable components and overall separating the logic from the components themselves.
